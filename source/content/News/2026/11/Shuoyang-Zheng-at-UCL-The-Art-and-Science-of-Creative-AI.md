Over 50 speakers from across art, science, technology and the creative industries will come together to debate what AI means for creativity, practice and scientific discovery, and how artistic and creative perspectives can challenge and transform current trajectories of AI.

[Shuoyang Zheng](https://www.creativeai.org.uk/#speaker-shuoyang-zheng) will be giving a talk on Saturday 14 November 15:30-16:45 on ["Generative AI in a space of crafting and hacking"](https://www.creativeai.org.uk/talk/generative-ai-in-a-space-of-crafting-and-hacking) at [*The Art and Science of Creative AI*](https://www.creativeai.org.uk), a two-day international interdisciplinar symposium at University College London, UCL East, November 13-14, 2026, London, UK. 

**Abstract**

> Repositioning technologies in a crafting and hacking space, seeing them fuel, change, or break familiar practices, has been a long-standing strategy used by artists: to unpack the creative possibilities of tools in their hands. The deviation from productivity, efficiency, and other technocentric concerns has constantly pushed the boundary of our relationship with algorithms, digital software, and more recently, AI systems.

> As a researcher in music technology and an electronic music producer, Shuoyang Jasper Zheng studies AI systems as materials to craft musical interactions and expressions, to understand how artistic practices impact technological advances. In his PhD research, he looked at neural audio synthesis (a family of generative AI techniques that use deep learning models trained on recordings to generate new sound) through this lens of crafting and hacking, with a focus on enabling broader participation in technological advances, from sound and music communities.

> In this talk, we will take a deep dive into neural audio synthesis, and how artist-researchers at the intersection of AI and the arts tweak, craft, and break these systems to explore their use in interactive sound and music systems. We will look at a range of creative applications that emerge in this crafting and hacking space, and implications for new notations and performance techniques with generative AI.

**Bio**

> Shuoyang Zheng (Jasper) (he/him) is a researcher and ML engineer at the Centre for Digital Music, Queen Mary University of London, funded by the UKRI Centre for Doctoral Training in AI and Music. His work explores AI systems through their convergence with media and arts, primarily focusing on the development of neural audio synthesis tools and on the understanding of how these technological advances interact with artistic practices. Shuoyang has been an Associate Lecturer at UAL Creative Computing Institute, teaching units across creative computing, data science and AI, and machine intelligence. During the 2020 lockdown, he wrote songs and produced tracks under the name "Alaska Winter". As an interdisciplinary artist-researcher, his works have been exhibited in venues such as Sónar+D Festival, IRCAM Forum, and various academic conferences.

The Art and Science of Creative AI symposium also marks the launch of the UCL Network for Creative AI, creating a new interdisciplinary platform for collaboration, research and exchange across UCL and beyond.
